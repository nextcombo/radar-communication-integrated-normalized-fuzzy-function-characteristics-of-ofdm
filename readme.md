# [Radar Communication Integrated]Normalized Fuzzy Function Characteristics of OFDM, OTFS, AFDM and CP-AFDM in ISAC [with MATLAB]

Germany's Universität Konstanz, Ructor University  
Abstract: This paper provides a unified and system-independent analysis of the fuzzy function (AF) characteristics of four representative multicarrier waveforms: Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM), Orthogonal Time-Frequency Spatial (OTFS), Amplitude Modulation Frequency Division Multiplexing (AFDM), and Continuous Wavelet Transform AFDM (CP-AFDM). These waveforms are considered key candidates for ISAC in future sixth-generation (6G) networks. The fuzzy functions of each waveform were directly obtained from their discrete time definitions and optimized through ideal fractional interpolation, enabling precise descriptions of their continuous time delay - Doppler response. The study examined two signal modes: one for communication, using random information symbols, suitable only for single-station scenarios; and another for perception, using fixed monomodal symbols, applicable to general multi-station scenarios. Additionally, normalized delay-Doppler units were used to evaluate the fuzzy functions and fuzzy metrics (including 3dB main lobe width, Peak Sidelobe Ratio (PSLR), and Integrated Sidelobe Ratio (ISLR)), ensuring direct conversion of these characteristics into any physical system configuration defined by bandwidth, sampling frequency, or symbol duration, while also ensuring intuitive and consistent comparison across different waveforms. The research results establish a unified benchmark for comparing waveform perceptual capabilities in ISAC design and clarify known characteristics: OFDM has excellent delay resolution and sidelobe properties but poor Doppler response; whereas advanced waveforms achieve better balance between delay and Doppler resolutions, with varying sidelobe characteristics. The paper publicly shares simulation code for smooth fuzzy functions to enhance the reproducibility of the study and supports future research on ISAC waveforms.  
Keywords: Fuzzy Function; Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM); Orthogonal Time-Frequency Spatial (OTFS); Amplitude Modulation Frequency Division Multiplexing (AFDM); Continuous Wavelet Transform AFDM (CP-AFDM); Delay-Doppler Analysis; ISAC
1. Introduction The future of wireless networks is expected to integrate communication and perception functions within a single radio framework, utilizing universal waveforms, hardware platforms, and signal processing chains to achieve synchronized information transmission and surrounding environment perception [1][2]. This emerging paradigm is widely referred to as Integrated Sensing and Communications (ISAC), one of the core pillars in the sixth-generation (6G) system definition and requirements [3][4].
Radar-centric ISAC solutions employ waveforms optimized for radar operations, such as Frequency Modulated Continuous Wave (FMCW), which are embedded with information while preserving perception capabilities through research exploring their application potential [5][6]. However, these solutions have significant limitations: poor compatibility with existing communication standards, and often failing to meet the high data rate requirements of 6G [7].
Given these limitations, communication-centric ISAC solutions, with their inherent high spectral efficiency, excellent communication performance, compatibility with existing standards, and flexibility in waveform design, become ideal choices for seamless integration [8][9].
Among these waveforms, Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) is a natural benchmark solution, currently the mainstream modulation method in wireless systems, with extensive research on OFDM ISAC [10][11]. Studies have shown that OFDM has excellent delay resolution (target distance) and sidelobe characteristics [12], but it has serious deficiencies in Doppler resolution (target velocity). In contrast, various advanced waveforms proposed for 6G, such as Orthogonal Time-Frequency Space (OTFS) [13][14], Amplified Frequency Division Multiplexing (AFDM) [15][16] and Linearly Modulated Frequency Shifted AFDM (CP-AFDM) [17], possess stronger delay-Doppler positioning capabilities, indicating they have highly attractive characteristics in perception applications, especially speed estimation.
Based on this, it is necessary to conduct a unified and systematic comparison of the characteristics and perceptual performance of these waveforms, with the Fuzzy Function (AF) being the key tool to achieve this goal. The fuzzy function is an inherent attribute of the transmitted waveform, unrelated to propagation channels and system configurations. It directly reflects the waveform's distance-velocity resolution and sidelobe characteristics through metrics such as main lobe width, peak sidelobe ratio (PSLR), and integral sidelobe ratio (ISLR) [18][19].
Despite numerous studies analyzing the fuzzy function of individual waveforms [20]-[22], these analyses are largely isolated and lack a unified framework for consistent comparison of waveforms in both delay and Doppler domains, approaching real continuous-time behavior. Therefore, this paper establishes a discrete-time fuzzy function evaluation framework that is independent of physical systems, providing a unified analysis for various candidate waveforms. This framework employs normalized delay and Doppler coordinates, combined with parameters such as physical bandwidth, sampling frequency, or symbol duration, facilitating easy conversion of analytical results into any actual system configuration.
Furthermore, to approximate the continuous-time behavior, this paper optimizes the fuzzy function through delayed domain interpolation and Doppler domain oversampling. The results are smooth and high-resolution, which can be used for visualization presentation as well as quantitative measurement evaluation. Finally, this paper also considers two signal patterns to make a comprehensive comparison of all waveforms: one is the generalized integrated mode using random information symbols, and the other is the perceptual priority mode using deterministic single-mode symbols.

4. Simulation Results and Analysis
Based on the aforementioned waveform formulas and fuzzy function analysis framework, this paper numerically simulates the fuzzy function characteristics of four candidate waveforms: OFDM, OTFS, AFDM, and CP-AFDM, and discusses the results. Since the simulation results are normalized, they are independent of the system configuration and can be directly converted into any actual system setup, ensuring fairness and consistency in comparisons between different types of waveforms. Additionally, smoothing and interpolation techniques generate high-resolution fuzzy function surfaces and sections, allowing for precise extraction of key fuzzy metric indicators without relying on the underlying discrete sample quantity N [1].
This analysis considers two signal scenarios: the first is a generic random symbol scenario, where the transmitted symbols represent information flow from a complex modulation constellation. This scenario reflects the actual integrated communication and perception mode, where waveforms need to simultaneously transmit information and perform perception tasks. In this scenario, the fuzzy function represents the average behavior during typical data transmission, providing a realistic evaluation basis for perception performance when communication and perception occur simultaneously.
However, the inherent randomness of the transmitted symbols affects the related structure of the waveform, thereby changing the shape and relevant metrics of the fuzzy function. Moreover, this mode is only applicable to sensors (i.e., receivers) that can perfectly capture the transmitted symbols (to perform matched filtering to obtain radar responses). In practical integrated communication and perception scenarios, this situation only holds true under single-station configurations, not universally applicable.
Therefore, to accommodate more general multi-station scenarios and to reveal the core characteristics of the waveform structure itself (unaffected by data randomness), this paper also studies the second scenario - the single-modulus symbol scenario (or purely perception scenario), where all transmitted symbols are set to 1. In practical applications, such purely perception operations can be realized through allocating specific time-frequency resources or pilot blocks within integrated communication and perception frames for dedicated perception transmission.

In this scenario, the four waveforms exhibit distinct differences. The zero Doppler cross-section in Figure 3 shows that OFDM, AFDM, and CP-AFDM have their main lobes concentrated, but their sidelobe characteristics are different; whereas, OTFS has a relatively wide main lobe. The sidelobe level of OFDM is the lowest, which is consistent with its well-known excellent delay resolution characteristic; AFDM appears to have higher sidelobe peaks near the normalized delay ±0.5, while the CP-AFDM variant effectively alleviates this issue.
Instead, the zero-latency cross-section in Figure 4 clearly shows the limited Doppler resolution of OFDM - its main lobe is almost flat. In stark contrast, both AFDM and CP-AFDM achieve clear and narrow main lobes, while OTFS also has a distinct Doppler response but with a broader main lobe. In terms of Doppler domain characteristics, AFDM performs best, with monotonically decaying sidelobe envelope; followed by CP-AFDM and OTFS, whose sidelobes are relatively stronger.
It should be noted that these characteristics hold for all valid parameter configurations across various waveforms. Of course, there are differences in adjustable parameters among different waveforms: OFDM has no adjustable parameters; OTFS has adjustable parameters for delay-Doppler grid dimensions; AFDM has adjustable parameters for linear frequency modulation (LFM); and CP-AFDM has additional adjustable parameters besides LFM, including the permutation index i. This result not only demonstrates the robustness of the observed fuzzy characteristics but also reflects the flexibility of these advanced waveforms in adapting to different configurations while still maintaining their basic delay-Doppler properties.

V. Conclusion This paper proposes a unified, system-independent framework for comparing the fuzzy function characteristics of four waveforms: OFDM, OTFS, AFDM, and CP-AFDM in two signal modes: random symbols and single-mode symbols. The framework enhances resolution by evaluating discrete time fuzzy functions, combining window ed-sinc delay interpolation with dense Doppler sampling, and reporting normalized cross-sectional metrics (3dB width, PSLR, ISLR) that facilitate fair and transferable comparisons between different systems. The method proposed in this paper provides a straightforward reference for integrated transduction waveform selection, clearly demonstrating and comparing the inherent delay-Doppler characteristics of each candidate waveform.
